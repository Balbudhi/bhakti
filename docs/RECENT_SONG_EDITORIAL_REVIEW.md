## What changed

- Gurbāṇī: repaired relative-clause attachment, self-offering predicates, indefinite expressions and missing English subjects/objects. The Creator placing Himself within the Guru is an explicitly chosen contextual reading of the Punjabi commentary. Kept the weighing and shore images.
- Tamil: restored full-moon modifier attachment, corrected participles and the singing subject, and made the food/ghee passage a connected English sentence. Kept the concrete drum instead of substituting theological commentary.
- Telugu/Sanskrit: rendered the soothing call as “Hush,” selected “imperishable bliss” for the compound in its parallel context, repaired the speech/gold question and caller, clarified the squirrel-devotion clause, and corrected compound and locative word links.
- Kōḷaṟu: corrected compound segmentation, grouped the stag-mounted goddess’s descriptions, resolved the ash/debate subject, removed invented pollen, clarified numbered asterisms, translated the plant name, and preserved repeated “good” consistently. A throat image was retained after a primary witness supported its metonymy; unusual English alone is not grounds to erase an image.
